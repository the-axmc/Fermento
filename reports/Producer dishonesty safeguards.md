# Hold back money, outsource the vouching

Fermento's biggest protection against a dishonest producer will not come from a sensor or a chip. It will come from **holding back part of the sale money** and **having someone other than the producer confirm that each batch exists**. Every wine and whisky platform reviewed keeps the goods with an independent warehouse that keeps its own ownership records, and the big frauds (Bordeaux Cellars, Cask Spirits Global, Oeno, Nant) all happened where buyers relied on paperwork from the seller. Fermento's model drops that safeguard by design: the producer is paid 100% at fill and keeps the batch in their own cellar, so buyers can lose everything on day one, the producer can lift the batch's price by up to 20% with self-scored check-ins, and a curator setting moves the price between 0.5x and 2.0x. Most fixes are cheap and fit the current contracts: pay the producer in tranches (about half at fill, the rest after inspections and at redemption); give an independent attestor a role in the registry so no offering opens without their signature; replace the copyable `sha256(NFC serial)` binding with NXP NTAG 424 DNA tags whose keys Fermento holds; stop check-ins from raising the price unless they carry evidence; and move the sensor key into a tamper-resistant chip that Fermento provisions, which Soroban can already check with `secp256r1_verify`. Portugal helps, because its appellation bodies (IVDP for Port, IVBAM for Madeira, CVR Península de Setúbal for Moscatel) and Kiwa Sativa (the certifier for Serra da Estrela cheese) already keep stock accounts, numbered seals or serial-numbered marks that limit how much a producer can legally sell. One step comes before all of this: legal advice, because a fractional, price-marked share in a batch that someone else manages looks more like a regulated investment than a sale of goods.

## Every platform that failed let the seller vouch for the goods

The serious platforms share one pattern: **the custodian is separate from the seller, and title lives in the custodian's ledger, not on a certificate the seller prints**. Cult Wines keeps each client's wine at Coterie Vaults, a bonded warehouse, assigned to a named client and "not part of Cult Wines' company assets", and sends monthly assignment reports to a third-party escrow provider for independent ownership checks ([Cult Wines](https://www.wineinvestment.com/us/how-it-works/where-is-your-wine/)). Liv-ex opens a storage account for each member at authorised warehouses and updates it with every trade ([Liv-ex membership terms](https://www.liv-ex.com/liv-ex-membership-terms/)). Scotch whisky must legally mature in an HMRC-approved warehouse, and title passes by a delivery order addressed to the warehouse keeper ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2024/01/scotch-whisky-a-guide-to-the-regulatory-framework-article)); since 3 March 2025 the warehousekeeper's own record has been the main check ([Whiskey Wash](https://thewhiskeywash.com/whiskey-articles/whisky-cask-ownership-just-changed-forever-wowgr-regulations-abolished-after-25-years/)). Even the tokenised spirits platforms BlockBar and BAXUS keep the bottles behind their NFTs in bonded or insured vaults ([Whiskey Wash](https://thewhiskeywash.com/whiskey-news/glenfiddich-blockbar-to-auction-single-cask-whisky-nft-to-be-redeemed-for-bottles/); [Alcohol Professor](https://www.alcoholprofessor.com/blog-posts/baxus-spirits-nft)). The research found **no platform that lets the producer keep the goods unchecked after being paid**. Fermento would be the first.

The fraud record shows why that matters, and most cases were phantom goods rather than bad wine. Bordeaux Cellars raised about **$100m from roughly 140 investors** on loans supposedly backed by bonded wine; the wine, the storage and the borrowers were fictitious, and its architect was sentenced to 10 years in April 2026 ([DHS/HSI](https://www.dhs.gov/news/2026/04/23/hsi-announces-sentencing-british-alien-nearly-100-million-ponzi-scheme-involving); [The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/04/uk-national-jailed-for-10-years-over-us97m-fine-wine-fraud/)). When Cask Spirits Global was wound up in August 2026, only **4 of 17 customers held valid ownership documents**, and a Scottish warehouse the firm had named denied any link to it ([The Spirits Business](https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2026/08/scam-whisky-firm-shut-down-by-uk-government/)). Oeno collapsed with about 2,600 investors and **only about 20% of its wine in individual customer accounts**; the rest sat in an account the company controlled ([The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2026/03/court-warns-of-lengthy-process-winding-down-oenofutures/)). Its 2023 monthly reports showed portfolios up 20%, yet investors who tried to sell found them down about 30%, a warning about price marks the seller can influence ([67 Pall Mall](https://www.67pallmall.com/under-the-surface-why-wine-investment-scams-keep-happening/)). The closest match to Fermento's double-selling risk is Nant Whisky in Tasmania, which **sold the same barrels several times over**; nobody noticed until new owners audited the stock in 2017 ([Mark Littler](https://www.marklittler.com/learning-from-whisky-scams/)).

Counterfeit cases teach a different lesson. **The producer's own records exposed Rudy Kurniawan**: Domaine Ponsot Clos Saint-Denis bottles dated 1945–1971 could not exist, because Ponsot only began making that wine in 1982 ([Wine Diarist](https://winediarist.com/?p=204)). That is the case for an on-chain batch record that cannot be backdated. Portugal has its own cases. In November 2025 ASAE, the food and economic safety authority, found a hidden bottling line in Vila Real with **1,326 bottles falsely labelled Douro DOC** and 12,000 fake labels ([The Portugal News](https://www.theportugalnews.com/news/2025-11-17/illegal-wine-operation-closed-down/918767)). A month later the "Puro Verde" investigation **arrested four members of the Vinho Verde regional commission itself** ([Food Safety News](https://www.foodsafetynews.com/2025/12/investigations-target-meat-honey-and-wine-sectors-in-portugal/)). Even an official certifier can be compromised, so no single attestor should be enough on its own.

Failure without fraud is a separate risk. Vint, a US platform that sold fractional wine shares under the SEC's Reg A+ rules, is winding down in 2026: its assets performed, but compliance costs sank it ([Angel Investors Network](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/alternative-investments/vint-wine-platform-winddown-2026)). Fermento's records should therefore outlive Fermento, sitting on chain where anyone can read them without the app, with a written plan for what happens to the batches if the company stops.

## Fermento's current contracts leave all six threats mostly open

Reading the repository against the six threats shows where the exposure sits. None of these are coding bugs; they are trust assumptions that suited a demo and need replacing before real money flows.

| Threat | What the code does today | The gap |
|---|---|---|
| Disappearing with the money | `buy()` sends 100% of the payment "straight to the producer" ([batch_shares](/Users/andlopvic/Desktop/Fermento/contracts/batch_shares/src/contract.rs)) | Buyers carry years of unsecured risk, with nothing to claw back |
| Double-selling or swapping a batch | `link_tag` stores `sha256(serial)`, one tag per batch ([non_fungible](/Users/andlopvic/Desktop/Fermento/contracts/non_fungible/src/contract.rs)) | Any phone reads the serial and cheap "magic" tags copy it, so the hash proves nothing. **The producer authorises the link and can re-link to a new tag at any time** |
| Fake sensor readings | `post_reading` checks an ed25519 signature against a key the curator sets ([condition_oracle](/Users/andlopvic/Desktop/Fermento/contracts/condition_oracle/src/contract.rs)) | Proves which key signed, not where the sensor is. A producer holding the key or device can sign anything, or put the sensor in a fridge |
| Fake check-ins | `post_checkin` needs only the producer's signature and a self-chosen `score` of 0–100 | A score of 100 each day adds 2.5% a day, **reaching the +20% cap in 8 days**, and one post every 14 days holds it there, for the cost of transaction fees |
| Misreporting volume | The issuer sets `total_shares` at `open_offering` | Nothing ties it to a regulator's stock account or an independent measurement |
| Inferior units at redemption | `redeem` burns shares and records a claim | No reference sample, seal-number check or quality check on delivery, and the producer has already been paid |

Two more levers deserve attention. **The curator-set producer rating multiplies the whole price by 0.5x to 2.0x**, and with a single pilot producer nothing can check it, which makes it the largest manipulable number in the system. **`set_maturity` lets the issuer move the maturity date at will**, so if Fermento's admin keys were compromised or colluded with a producer, holders would get no warning.

## Hardware proves which chip spoke, not that the cask is real

**Secure-element sensors** are chips that keep the signing key locked inside, so nobody can copy or extract it. Soroban already has the check built in: `secp256r1_verify` validates P-256 signatures on chain, alongside `ed25519_verify` and `secp256k1_recover` ([soroban-sdk crypto](https://www.mintlify.com/stellar/rs-soroban-sdk/api/modules/crypto)). The cheapest suitable chip, the Microchip ATECC608B, signs only with P-256 and costs **US$1.36–3.89 as a bare chip** ([LCSC](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C1020341.html)) or **US$4.95 on a plug-in board** ([Adafruit](https://www.adafruit.com/product/4314)); keeping ed25519 would mean NXP's SE050, and only some variants support it ([NXP SE050 datasheet](https://cn.nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/SE050-DATASHEET.pdf)). A pilot node pairs an ESP32 microcontroller, an ATECC608B that creates its key internally where it can never be read out, and a temperature and humidity sensor, for a research-team estimate of **€30–60 in parts**. Two contract caveats apply: the soroban-sdk crypto API has been reorganised before, so check the exact function shape against the SDK version in use ([rs-soroban-env PR #1376](https://github.com/stellar/rs-soroban-env/pull/1376)), and test whether Soroban rejects some valid P-256 signatures (the "low-S" rule), which the research could not confirm. No off-the-shelf cellar logger was found that signs its readings; common LoRaWAN loggers only encrypt data on its way to the vendor's cloud ([JRI SPY TH](https://www.directindustry.com/prod/jri/product-67888-2827302.html)).

**A secure chip proves which chip signed, not that the reading is true.** So **Fermento, not the producer, must provision the chip and register its key**, or the producer simply registers a key on a device they control, and the sensor must be physically tied to the cask. No commercial product does that cryptographically, but cheap physical methods work. A University of Adelaide sensor sitting in the bung measured the gap above the wine and the wine's temperature for **about AUD 27 in parts, with at least 12 months of battery life** ([Sensors 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4570382/)). A whisky patent puts a load cell and an alcohol vapour sensor under each cask and cites an expected evaporation loss ("angel's share") of **about 2% a year in Scotland and up to 5% elsewhere** ([US11954554](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11954554)). Weight is hard to fake without the real cask: a genuine ageing cask loses weight steadily, a sudden rise means topping up or dilution, and a sensor sitting in a fridge shows no weight curve at all. Cheap software checks raise the bar further. A real cask in a cellar shows small, delayed daily temperature swings compared with outdoor weather, while a fridge shows a flat line or a compressor's regular cycle; a second sensor on the cask's outer face and an accelerometer that reports movement add more channels to fake. These are design inferences rather than tested products, but together they raise the cost of cheating from "move one sensor" to "simulate a whole cask across several signed channels".

**Anti-clone tags** are the weakest link today and the cheapest to fix. An **NXP NTAG 424 DNA** tag produces a fresh cryptographic code (an AES-CMAC) on every tap, computed over the tag ID and a counter that rises with each read, so a copied tag can only repeat an old code ([WakDev](https://www.wakdev.com/en/knowledge-base/nfc-chips/nxp-ntag424-dna.html)). Replays are blocked by rejecting any counter that is not higher than the last one seen ([docs.rs ntag424](https://docs.rs/ntag424)), and the **TagTamper** variant adds a wire loop that reports whether a seal has been opened ([NXP](https://nxp.com/products/rfid-nfc/nfc-hf/ntag-for-tags-labels/ntag-424-dna-424-dna-tagtamper-advanced-security-and-privacy-for-trusted-iot-applications:NTAG424DNA)). The code relies on a shared secret key, so it **must be checked off chain by whoever holds that key**: Fermento or an auditor, never the producer, who could otherwise program fresh valid tags for any cask. The checker then posts the batch, tag hash, counter and tamper state to Soroban, signed with its registered key. Prices are unsettled, from about **£1.66 per card** at UK retail ([usmartcards](https://usmartcards.co.uk/ntag-424-dna)) down to a Chinese sticker sample quoted at US$0.11, though the exact chip in that sample is unclear ([RFID Tag World](https://www.rfidtagworld.com/products/Self-destructive-tag/Tamper-HF-NFC-Tag/nfc-ndef.html)). Arx HaLo chips are an alternative that can be checked entirely on chain: they sign a caller-supplied challenge with secp256k1, which `secp256k1_recover` verifies, and each carries a manufacturer attestation ([libhalo](https://github.com/arx-research/libhalo/blob/master/docs/halo-command-set.md)), though no public pricing was found. Wine already uses the pattern: Selinko's capsule seals put an NFC chip under the capsule that reports whether the bottle was opened or the cork pierced ([Labels & Labeling](https://labelsandlabeling.com/news/interactive-technology/selinko-and-toppan-launch-new-protection-against-wine-bottle-refilling)).

**Oracles on Stellar** do not solve this. The providers listed for Stellar (RedStone, Reflector, Band, DIA, Pyth and Chainlink) all supply market prices, and none mentions IoT data ([Stellar Docs](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/data/oracles/oracle-providers)). Chainlink's price feeds went live on Stellar mainnet on 14 October 2026 ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/stellar-integrates-chainlink-data-services-defi/)) and are useful for EUR/USD valuations, but **no third-party oracle adds trust to cellar evidence**; routing readings through one would just add a middleman. The right design is the one Fermento already half-has: signed readings relayed to a contract that checks them against a registered device key and enforces simple rules, namely sequence numbers that only go up, timestamp windows, plausible ranges and a maximum gap between readings. A later option is a zero-knowledge proof that a month of readings matches local weather. Soroban can check such proofs since Protocol 25 added BN254 pairing ([Stellar X-Ray](https://stellar.org/blog/developers/announcing-stellar-x-ray-protocol-25)), and a RISC Zero verifier exists for it ([Boundless](https://boundless.network/blog/verifying-proofs-on-stellar)), but that belongs after the pilot.

## Portugal's regulators already keep ledgers that cap overselling

Fermento's best independent witnesses already exist, and they are state or state-delegated bodies. IVDP keeps a **"conta corrente" (stock account) for every Port and Douro trader**, and Douro sales are limited to the certified volume of each batch ([IVDP Guia Cap. 2](https://www.ivdp.pt/pt/comunicacao/guias-tematicos/comercializacao/guia-comercializacap/capitulo-2-colocacao-no-mercado/)). It also issues **numbered guarantee seals**, and traders must log each seal's bottling date, brand, type, quantity and lot number ([IVDP Circular 148](https://www.ivdp.pt/pt/docs/circulares/circular_148.pdf)). Beyond Port, **every holder of wine in Portugal must file an annual stock declaration with IVV**, referenced to 31 July, on the SIVV system, with fines up to €10,000 ([postal.pt](https://postal.pt/economia/quem-tiver-vinho-armazenado-tera-de-fazer-esta-declaracao-ate-setembro-ou-arrisca-multa-de-10-mil-euros/)). IVBAM seals every bottle of Madeira and runs an EU-designated official lab and a tasting panel ([JORAM 2023](https://joram.madeira.gov.pt/joram/1serie/Ano%20de%202023/ISerie-070-2023-04-13sup2.pdf)), and it can suspend trading if a producer's stock falls below a required minimum ([RAM Official Gazette 2015](https://madeirawijn.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Madeira-wijnwetgeving.pdf)). CVR Península de Setúbal is the designated body that certifies DO Setúbal wines, including Moscatel ([Portaria 793/2009](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/detalhe/portaria/793-2009-492980)). For Serra da Estrela cheese, Kiwa Sativa certifies each cheese, which carries a **serial-numbered casein mark linked in the records to its milk lot** ([Caderno de Especificações 2022](https://tradicional.dgadr.gov.pt/images/prod_imagens/queijos/docs/CE_Queijo_Serra_Estrela_2022.pdf)) and a serial-numbered hologram from the state mint, INCM ([DN](https://www.dn.pt/arquivo/diario-de-noticias/certificacao-garante-qualidade-do-queijo-da-serra-3701108.html)).

These ledgers have limits. **None has a public API or online seal lookup**, so Fermento can only reach the data through the producer, either as copies of the stock declaration and the quality certificate (CCQ) or through a mandate letting the body confirm a balance. They also **track volume per wine type, not per cask, and not who owns the economic rights**: they cap the total a producer can sell, but cannot show that a cask was not also sold privately. They are still the right ceiling for `total_shares`, and seal and casein-mark number ranges recorded on chain at onboarding make a strong check at redemption.

Excise rules do not supply a UK-style independent custodian. The Portuguese tax authority confirms that **small wine producers are exempt from needing a fiscal warehouse (entreposto fiscal)** ([AT Ofício-circulado 25106/2026](https://info-aduaneiro.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/legislacao_aduaneira/oficios_circulados_doclib/Documents/Oficio-circulado-25106-2026.pdf)), so many pilot producers will have no electronic excise movement records at all. The research also found **no Portuguese equivalent of London City Bond**, a third-party bonded store with private owner accounts. That leaves two realistic custody options: the producer's own cellar backed by regulator ceilings and Fermento-commissioned audits, or a bailment contract with a Gaia shipper or another licensed warehouse operator. Nobody was confirmed to offer the second as a service, but it is the closest thing to a UK delivery order.

Lab testing closes the swap-and-dilute gap but not the volume gap. Routine chemistry is widely available: IVDP's Porto lab is **ISO/IEC 17025 accredited** ([IVDP](https://www.ivdp.pt/pt/docs/acreditacoes/L0115A1(2022-11-30).pdf)) and IVBAM runs the official Madeira lab. The practical anti-swap protocol is a **reference sample drawn and sealed by an independent sampler at listing**, with its lab results hashed on chain and compared against a fresh sample at audit or redemption. Dilution shows up as drift in alcohol strength or density beyond normal ageing, and a younger or cheaper substitute shows up in the chemistry. Specialist tests cover the harder cases, with limits. Isotope methods (SNIF-NMR and IRMS) detect added sugar or water ([FIRMS 2019](https://openpub.fmach.it/retrieve/handle/10449/58773/97871/Book%20of%20abstract_FIRMS2019_Solovyev.pdf)), though one 2023 study reported only a **52.4% detection rate** on adulterated samples ([BIO Web of Conferences 2023](https://www.bio-conferences.org/articles/bioconf/pdf/2023/01/bioconf_oiv2022_02017.pdf)). Radiocarbon **dates post-1950s whisky to within 2–3 years** ([University of Glasgow](https://www.gla.ac.uk/research/az/suerc/news/headline_768202_en.html)), and the University of Minho has **DNA methods that detect cow milk in Serra da Estrela sheep cheese** ([UMinho](https://repositorium.sdum.uminho.pt/bitstream/1822/75157/1/document_55092_1.pdf)). **No lab method measures quantity.** Volume needs an independent inspector to dip or gauge the cask, with the result checked against the expected evaporation loss and the regulator's stock declaration. No per-test prices were found, so budget for quotes.

## Withheld money and retained shares deter cheating that cameras cannot see

Fake check-ins and quietly substituted units are hard to catch continuously, so the efficient answer is economic: **make cheating unprofitable when caught, and make getting caught likely enough**. The Bordeaux en primeur market shows that long-dated pre-sales need this. Buyers there pay about two years before delivery ([The Drinks Business](https://www.thedrinksbusiness.com/2006/05/fit-for-purpose-bordeaux-en-primeur/6/)), **Liv-ex requires sellers to post a bank guarantee or insurance bond** ([Liv-ex](https://liv-ex.com/?p=48375)), and after the Mayfair Cellars collapse Berry Bros & Rudd insured against merchant failure ([Decanter](https://www.decanter.com/?p=93923)). Fermento's buyers currently get neither escrow nor a guarantee.

The cheapest strong fix is **tranche escrow**: change `buy()` so payments go into the `batch_shares` contract instead of straight to the producer, release money in tranches when an attestor confirms each milestone, and add a refund function that lets holders burn shares for their share of any unreleased money if a deadline passes or a dispute is upheld. The research team estimates about 150 lines of Soroban and no new dependencies. A reasonable starting split, offered as a design proposal rather than a market benchmark, is **50% at fill, 15% after each of two independent inspections, and 20% at redemption**, which respects the producer's front-loaded costs while keeping a real stake in delivering good units. Trustless Work, an escrow service built on Stellar, offers a ready-made alternative with separate roles for marking a milestone, approving it, releasing funds and resolving disputes ([Trustless Work](https://docs.trustlesswork.com/trustless-work/introduction/technology-overview)). It fits many small buyers purchasing shares at different times less naturally, and its fee and audit status were not found.

Skin in the game comes next. The strongest form costs the producer no cash: **lock 10–15% of each batch's shares in the contract until redemption is complete**, so a producer who double-sells or delivers poor units loses their own slice of the batch. A small bond adds to it, for example 5% withheld from the first tranche, which can be cut ("slashed") on a missed deadline, an upheld dispute or tamper evidence. DeFi lending shows the limits of bonds. Maple Finance's first-loss cover recovered only about $2.5m of a roughly $36m default ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/192097/maple-finance-default-orthogonal-trading)), and Goldfinch's worst losses came from off-chain misuse of funds, such as a borrower moving money to an affiliate without authorisation ([DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/defi/african-motorbike-finance-company-defaults-on-loan/)). **On-chain bonds cap the loss, but they do not replace physical audits or legal recourse.**

Random audits make the deterrence work. The penalty times the chance of an audit has to exceed the gain from cheating. If double-selling 10% of a €20,000 batch gains €2,000 and the chance of an audit in a year is 25%, the penalty must be at least €8,000 (€2,000 ÷ 0.25). Retained shares, the bond and withheld tranches can add up to that; a bond alone cannot. The practical tools borrow UMA's "accept unless challenged" model ([UMA docs](https://docs.uma.xyz/protocol-overview/how-does-umas-oracle-work)), built directly in Soroban because neither UMA nor Kleros runs on Stellar. Random challenge check-ins give the producer 48 hours to submit a photo showing a one-time code written next to the tagged cask, plus a fresh tag tap. Random physical audits are funded from storage fees. Any holder can post a USDC deposit to call an inspection, refunded with a bounty if the inspector finds a problem. Milestones release automatically unless someone challenges them within 7 days, in which case a named human dispute resolver decides.

The same logic fixes check-ins and the rating. Check-ins should stop adding any price premium and only prevent the existing decay, unless they carry an evidence hash with a challenge code or a second signature from a Fermento sensor, a visiting holder who tapped the tag, or an independent oenologist. The rating should come from verified outcomes (on-time deliveries, inspections passed, sensor uptime) rather than curator judgement. Fermento already uses OpenZeppelin's Stellar library, which includes a **timelock** that can delay admin actions such as `set_maturity`, `set_rating` and tranche releases so holders see them coming ([Stellar Docs: OpenZeppelin](https://developers.stellar.org/docs/tools/developer-tools/openzepplin-contracts)). Stellar attestation projects such as ACTA exist, but their maturity is unverified ([SCF](https://communityfund.stellar.org/project/acta-720)), so a small in-house attestor role is simpler for a pilot.

Insurance is a backstop, not a fraud control. Stock insurance covers physical loss and **does not prove ownership** ([Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2025/03/21/the-risks-and-safeguards-in-whisky-cask-investment)), and it will not pay out for fraud by the insured producer. No insurance protocol on Stellar was found. The feasible pilot version is a platform guarantee pool, funded by perhaps 1–2% of each sale and paid out under published rules, which avoids the improvised rescues Goldfinch's backers had to make ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/rwa-protocol-goldfinch-writes-down-usd7m-of-usd20m-loan-to-usd0)).

## The regulatory question decides which safeguards are mandatory

This caveat comes first in practice, even though it sits last here. **A Fermento share is a fraction of a batch, its return depends on someone else's ageing work, the money goes to the producer up front, and many people hold it at once.** That matches the elements regulators use to identify a collective investment scheme or a financial instrument better than it matches a sale of goods. In the UK, whisky casks stay outside financial regulation only because each cask is individually allocated to a named owner; pooled or managed arrangements risk being unregulated collective investment schemes ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e8f44116-4a5a-4c33-944b-6aa0d5c4f280); [FSMA s.235](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/235)). In the EU, ESMA's March 2025 guidelines set out when crypto-assets count as **financial instruments under MiFID II**, and those that do not fall under MiCA ([Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/lu/en/Industries/investment-management/perspectives/ESMA-clarifies-which-crypto-assets-might-qualify-as-financial-instruments-in-the-EU.html)). Portugal's crowdfunding law (Lei 102/2015) requires CMVM registration for equity and loan platforms ([OCC](https://occ.pt/pt-pt/noticias/crowdfunding-0)), and no CMVM or ESMA statement specific to tokenised wine, cheese or other ageing goods was found. Fermento's live price mark, care bonus and rating multiplier all push it further toward "investment".

The lowest-regret path is to get written advice from Portuguese counsel, and ideally an informal view from CMVM, before any public sale; to limit the pilot to known or sophisticated buyers, or a small capped pre-sale; and to consider framing the token as a forward purchase of specific units from a named batch, closer to en primeur. That framing may keep it a consumer sale, but EU consumer law and alcohol excise rules then apply. Whatever the classification, regulators will expect independent existence checks, escrowed funds, audits and a registry that outlives the platform, so adopting them voluntarily costs nothing in any scenario. One smaller rule: the EU wine e-label QR page **must not contain marketing or tracking** ([CCCI summary](https://ccci.org.cy/?p=29897)), so Fermento's provenance page needs its own tag or link.

The plan rests on some unknowns. The research found no current EU prices for NTAG 424 DNA TagTamper stickers, Arx HaLo chips, lab tests, or audits by an oenologist or certifier, and no commercial cask load cell or smart bung confirmed as shipping today. Whether IVDP, CVRPS, IVBAM or Kiwa Sativa would confirm a producer's balance directly, or act as an on-chain attestor, is unknown and needs a direct conversation. No fraud cases or authentication methods were found for presunto (cured ham), and no rules specific to Portuguese whisky were researched.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "How do we make sensors trustworthy?" matters less than "who besides the producer vouches for the batch, and how much of the producer's money waits on that vouching?" Sensors, tags and labs make lying expensive, but every fraud in the record succeeded because the only witness was the seller and the money had already moved. Fermento's on-chain registry can be the tamper-proof memory that Kurniawan's victims lacked, but it only becomes evidence when an independent key signs it, and the producer's payout only becomes an incentive while part of it is still waiting.

There is also a strategic upside. Portugal's appellation system already runs per-producer stock accounts, numbered seals and accredited labs, which UK cask investors never had. A pilot that reconciles each batch with IVDP or CVR records, keeps the tag keys in Fermento's hands and escrows part of the payment would be stricter than most of the platforms that failed, at a cost of a few euros per cask in hardware and a few hundred lines of Soroban. The open risks are legal, not technical: the classification question, and whether a regulator will confirm balances directly, need answers before the first euro moves.
